Task 3. Replace the duplicate FAQs and add new ones Theme

The FAQ currently repeats "What is biological motion?" and "How far away can drivers see reflective gear?" from the sections above it. Keep one version of each and add these:

Do I still need bike lights if I wear reflective gear?

Yes. Lights let drivers see you from angles their headlights don't reach, and many places require them. Reflective material on your ankles and wrists is what makes a driver recognize you as a person once their headlights hit you. Use both. Link: LED clip lights product, Night Kit.

Are reflective socks better than a reflective vest?

For being recognized at night, placement beats area. In a cyclist study, riders wearing ankle and knee reflectors with a vest were recognized over 3x farther than with the vest alone.2 In a pedestrian study, the same amount of reflective material on the ankles and wrists was recognized about 23x farther than on the chest (212.7 m vs 9.1 m).6 Link: reflective socks, reflective bands.

Does fluorescent yellow work at night?

No. Fluorescent color needs the UV in sunlight. Car headlights give off almost none, so fluorescent fabric looks like ordinary fabric at night. For night, you need retroreflective material. Link: /pages/retroreflective-vs-fluorescent

Where should I wear reflective material?

Start with your feet and ankles. They move the most when you run or pedal, which is the motion drivers recognize as human. Then add wrists and hands, then knees and elbows. Link: /pages/shop-by-body-zone

How do I wash reflective gear?

Reflective and fluorescent socks: machine wash cold. Our reflective socks are rated for 50+ washes. Jackets, vests and gloves: hand wash cold and hang dry. Summer cycling gloves: close the hook-and-loop straps before washing so they don't snag. Link: /blogs/news/wash-reflective-running-gear
