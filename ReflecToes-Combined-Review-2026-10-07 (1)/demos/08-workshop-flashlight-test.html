Is your gear actually reflective? Test it in 10 seconds.

You can't tell by looking. In daylight, retroreflective material looks like dull grey fabric. These two tests show you what a driver sees.

Test your own gear

Your phone's flash sees what a driver's headlights see.

Test 1 · easiest

The phone flash photo

  1. Hang or lay your gear in a dim room, or go outside after dark.
  2. Step back at least 10 ft.
  3. Take a photo with the flash forced on.

Why it works: the flash sits right next to the camera lens, just like headlights sit right below a driver's eyes. Reflective sends the light straight back into the lens.

Test 2 · the workshop demo

Flashlight at your eye

  1. Dim the room. Have a volunteer stand across it in their gear.
  2. Hold a flashlight right against your temple and look along the beam.
  3. Now hold it at your hip. Watch the reflective parts dim.

Why it works: reflective sends light back toward the light source. Your eyes only see the full glow when the light is right next to them, the way a driver's are.

What you'll see in the flash photo

RetroreflectiveBlazes white. This is what drivers see.
Fluorescent yellowLooks ordinary. No UV in a flash or headlights.
White cottonA little brighter, no glow.
Dark clothingNearly disappears.

Chips show daylight color, then the flash photo every few seconds. In a Clemson study, people predicted reflective swatches would look about half as bright as they did in a beam, and expected fluorescent yellow to be the brightest (King, Szubski & Tyrrell 2023).
